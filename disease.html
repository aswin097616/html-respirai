Common Respiratory Diseases and Sounds

Abnormal lung sounds provide vital clues for diagnosis. Our model is trained to distinguish between these key sound characteristics. [Image of the human lung]

1. Upper Respiratory Tract Infection (URTI)

a general term for an infection of the nose, throat, sinuses, larynx, or trachea. These are typically caused by viruses like the rhinovirus and result in common symptoms such as a sore throat, runny or stuffy nose, cough, and sneezing.

2. Asthma

A condition causing airway swelling and constriction. The signature sound is a 'high-pitched, musical wheeze', especially on expiration. In severe, acute attacks, breath sounds may be diminished ("silent chest") due to near-total obstruction.

3. Pneumonia

Lung infection filling air sacs with fluid. This typically presents with 'late-inspiratory fine crackles' (rales) over the affected lobe, and 'bronchial breath sounds' in areas of consolidation (fluid-filled tissue).

4. Bronchiectasis

Permanent widening of the airways leading to chronic mucus buildup. The characteristic sound is 'coarse, wet, and often persistent crackles' heard mainly over the affected areas.

5. Healthy Lungs

In a healthy patient, the model detects 'vesicular breath sounds'—soft, low-pitched sounds heard over most of the lung field—with no adventitious (abnormal) sounds like crackles or wheezes.
